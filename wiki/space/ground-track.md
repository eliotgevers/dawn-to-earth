## A projection on a rotating planet

A ground track is the path on Earth beneath a spacecraft as it travels. It projects a three-dimensional orbit onto a map. Earth rotates beneath many orbits, so two successive revolutions can produce shifted ground tracks even when the orbit is stable in a reference frame centered on Earth.

A ground track alone does not describe the observation footprint. The footprint depends on the instrument’s viewing geometry and orientation. A satellite can still observe terrain to the side of its track, and the track provides no evidence of whether clouds blocked the view. Distinguish a ground-track map from a map of actual observations before drawing conclusions about data coverage.

## Fictional example

A Kōtare Mapper classroom map uses one line for the predicted ground track and shaded areas for regions actually covered in the sample dataset.
