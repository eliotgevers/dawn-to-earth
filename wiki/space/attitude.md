## Which way is it facing?

Attitude describes a spacecraft’s orientation relative to a stated reference. A camera can face Earth, a panel can face the Sun, and an antenna can face a receiver. These statements describe directions, and they only make sense when you identify which spacecraft face or instrument is being discussed.

Pointing is a mission-level objective as well as a spacecraft function. An observation can be associated with a known position and still be unusable if the instrument was looking somewhere else. Conversely, a pointing observation says little about the shape of the spacecraft’s orbit. The reference frame is part of the meaning of any reported orientation.

## Fictional example

A Fern Pathfinder diagram labels the camera direction and the spacecraft body axes separately so a reviewer can interpret the same drawing.
