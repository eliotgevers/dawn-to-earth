## Height, shape, and orientation

Orbits are described by more than height. Two spacecraft at similar heights may see different parts of Earth because their orbital planes are tilted differently. The shape also matters: in an elliptical orbit a spacecraft’s distance from Earth changes over the course of a revolution.

| Orbit description | What the label describes | Civilian context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low Earth orbit | A relatively low range of orbital heights | Many Earth-observing missions |
| Polar orbit | An orbit whose plane passes near both poles | Repeated observations over many latitudes |
| Geostationary orbit | A circular equatorial orbit that appears fixed from the ground | Continuous viewing of a region |

The labels overlap: a satellite can be both in low Earth orbit and in a near-polar orbit.
