## Public product families

| Product | How Dawn describes it |
| --- | --- |
| CubeDrive | A self-contained spacecraft propulsion module for CubeSat-class missions. |
| SatDrive | A modular family for larger spacecraft, with packaged systems tailored to customer integration needs. |

Both are propulsion subsystems. They sit within a spacecraft that also contains the bus and mission payload. See [payloads and spacecraft buses](../systems/payload-bus.md) and [spacecraft interfaces](../systems/interfaces.md).

## Public lunar relay example

In November 2024, Dawn announced that it would provide propulsion modules for two lunar data-relay satellites. The announcement assigned microsatellite bus development and communications-payload integration to Blue Canyon Technologies. The purpose described was to relay communications between Earth and a lunar lander. Dawn also reported an electronics radiation evaluation completed earlier that year at NASA’s Space Radiation Laboratory at Brookhaven National Laboratory. This is the work the announcement described at that date.

The division of responsibilities gives readers a concrete example of why a subsystem supplier and spacecraft integrator need a common understanding of the interface. In the separate fictional portfolio, linking a build observation to an affected interface illustrates such a handoff. The records, software, and decisions in Dawn to Earth are invented.

## Public engineering software work

Dawn’s full-stack developer listing describes software work that includes API services, data visualization, testing infrastructure, and internal engineering applications, alongside collaboration with assembly, integration, and verification colleagues. Dawn to Earth uses those broad problem areas as inspiration for its offline record browser. The prototype’s data model, review roles, and decision states are independent design choices.
