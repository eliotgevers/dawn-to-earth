## Different questions at different stages

A mission starts with a need, such as collecting consistent observations of a region. Early work translates that need into objectives and candidate concepts. Later work defines the system, builds and integrates components, and gathers evidence that the product meets requirements and serves its intended purpose.

Each stage leaves a different kind of record. A concept describes intent, a baseline describes an agreed definition, and a build record describes a particular article. Reviews use those records together, especially where the design or mission intent has changed. A handoff keeps important evidence and open questions available to the next team.

## Fictional example

Kōtare Mapper’s concept explains why the observations matter. Its integration packet identifies the particular article used to collect a rehearsal dataset.
