## Can someone interpret it later?

A useful evidence record identifies the article or design in scope, the observation itself, and enough context to interpret it. A file may be present but lack a source identifier or revision. That is an evidence-quality issue, even if the file appears plausible.

A reviewer can decide that the evidence is relevant and usable without making a final judgment about the underlying requirement. Maintain that distinction in the record. If an observation only covers one interface, it does not establish results for unrelated interfaces. A link to the applicable requirement makes that boundary clearer.

## Fictional example

A Fern Pathfinder image identifies the enclosure but omits the witness in question. It is useful as a record of the enclosure and insufficient for the visibility claim.
