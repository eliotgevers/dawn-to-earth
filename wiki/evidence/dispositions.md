## Name the scope of the decision

A disposition is an explicit outcome from a review, tied to a record or identified subject. A design-level disposition and a capture-level disposition can coexist. When a group has different applicable outcomes, report those outcomes by serial rather than choosing one to represent the group.

Missing evidence, unreviewed evidence, and reviewed evidence are separate states. A grouped summary should show what is covered and make an open exception discoverable. A recorded acknowledgment adds to history but does not automatically supersede a decision. If one conclusion really replaces another, keep that relationship explicit.

## Fictional example

The locator lab shows one serial reviewed and another needing follow-up. Their shared revision does not turn those distinct outcomes into a group approval.
