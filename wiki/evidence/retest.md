## A new observation has its own identity

A retest or follow-up produces a new observation. It should link to the original item that prompted it and record any meaningful scope difference. Both records help readers see why a later decision was made and whether it applies to the original question.

A request for follow-up is different from completed work, and a completed observation is different from an accepted conclusion. A replacement can supersede an earlier artifact for a particular decision only when that relationship is established. A new result on another revision or serial does not silently alter an existing review.

## Fictional example

A fictional Kea follow-up asks for a missing view. Filing the new record leaves the request visible until a reviewer reaches a disposition about the pertinent evidence.
