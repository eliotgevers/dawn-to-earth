## Start with what is plotted

An evidence chart should identify the quantity and unit on each axis. Time may be an absolute timestamp, elapsed duration from a named event, or a sample index. These are different quantities. Make sure a visual comparison uses the same scales and applies to the same article or clearly identified different articles.

Gaps, smoothing, and normalization affect what a viewer can infer. A line across missing data can look like an observation when it is only a visual connection. A normalized response is useful for comparing shape but does not establish an absolute magnitude unless the normalization is recorded and appropriate.
