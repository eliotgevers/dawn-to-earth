## Write the unit into the expression

A conversion changes the unit used to express a quantity. For instance,

$$
2.5\,\mathrm{m} \times \frac{100\,\mathrm{cm}}{1\,\mathrm{m}}=250\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

Here m means metres and cm means centimetres. The fraction is a dimensionless representation of one, allowing metres to cancel. For time, one minute is 60 seconds. If $t_{\mathrm{s}}$ and $t_{\mathrm{min}}$ are the numerical values in seconds and minutes, respectively, then $t_{\mathrm{s}}=60t_{\mathrm{min}}$.

Some scales, including temperatures expressed in Celsius and kelvin, have an offset. Converting a reading may differ from converting a difference between readings. Keep the quantity, unit, and rounding context next to the value.

## Fictional example

A learning exercise converts 2 minutes to 120 seconds without changing the underlying duration.
