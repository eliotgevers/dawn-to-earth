## Temperature is a state, heat is transferred

Temperature describes the thermal state of a body. Heat transfer describes energy moving across a boundary. Inside a spacecraft, components can exchange heat through contact and radiation. Between the spacecraft and its surrounding vacuum, radiation is central to energy exchange.

Thermal conditions can change as the spacecraft moves between illumination and shadow, or as internal sources vary. A temperature observation is most useful when it identifies the place, unit, and relevant time context. A reading at one location cannot establish the temperature at a different point on an assembly. Keep an observation and the engineering interpretation of that observation separate.

## Fictional example

A Fern Pathfinder observation identifies which enclosure surface a classroom chart refers to, rather than labeling every line simply ‘spacecraft temperature.’
